# Reviewer 5

We thank the reviewer for their comments and suggestions and their acknowledgment that this dataset "fills a gap in publicly available, expert-annotated underwater video data”. 

> (1) The annotated dataset is relatively small, which may limit the robustness and generalizability of the model.

While our dataset is not the size of the largest behvaioral datasets that exist, the important and novel aspect of our dataset is that is generated by highly trained scientists using video recordings that were intended for scientific purposes. For this reason, we believe this dataset fills an important gap between small scientific datasets and very large web derived datasets. As can be noted in **Table 1** in the paper, WildFin (~9 hours) is comparable in size to other in-situ animal datasets like MammAlps (15 hours) and KABR (10 hours). Notably, fish typically come in larger groups than other species (especially in our context), therefore the average number of annotations per frame is actually much higher than in other in-situ datasets.

> (2) In terms of evaluation, the paper only reports classification results and lacks visualization or qualitative analysis, especially on unseen cases. This makes it difficult to assess the model’s generalization ability and whether the dataset is sufficiently comprehensive.

We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. Unfortunately, we are unable to show visualizations and qualitative results in the rebuttal, but we will be sure to include this in the revised paper. 

> (3) (Minor) The presentation quality, particularly in figure design and formatting, could be improved to enhance clarity and readability.

Thank you for the suggestion. We will be sure to improve the figures, tables, and clarity for the final version of the paper.
